Watch fear from above

Pick a card. Any card. Now watch it breathe.

Each star is a stock. Distance is disagreement: stocks that move together sit close; stocks that live their own lives keep their distance. When the market is well, the constellation is wide and lumpy, full of little tribes. When fear arrives, every star falls toward every other until twenty companies become one trembling object. Here is the trick nobody expects: press play on 2008 and the room starts closing in the summer of 2007 — a full year before Lehman — and stays closed while the index goes on to make new highs. Whether that is a warning or a coincidence is the question this whole site exists to test.

A stethoscope for a market

A small open-source machine takes the market's pulse every week: how much room the stocks keep, how tightly the tissue is bound, which way the capital is migrating. It drafts; a human verdicts. Nothing up the sleeve — the code is public. Look through the Observatory →

Honest by construction

A magician who shows you the trick going wrong.

The paper portfolio exists to test the theory, not to sell it. Eight trading days in, two stocks we'd written off are outrunning the ones we favored, and the 356.69 pair sits a hair under parity — the opposite of what we predicted. Eight days is weather, not climate. But it's already on the ledger, and it's already the subject of the first letter. That's the whole act: wonder in front, receipts behind.

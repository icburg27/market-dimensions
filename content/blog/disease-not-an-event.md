Ask anyone where the 2008 crisis happened and they'll give you a date: September 15, Lehman files, the world changes. That's how we remember it — as an *event*, a lightning strike, sudden and external.

The geometry remembers it differently. The geometry remembers an illness.

## The patient looked fine

In February 2007, the research panel's effective dimension read **10.04** — as much breathing room as the body ever showed in that era. Twenty assets living twenty distinguishable lives: energy doing energy things, staples doing staples things, tribes and gaps and disagreements everywhere. On the [manifold](/), it looks like a wide, lumpy constellation. A body at ease.

Prices agreed. The index was grinding higher. By every conventional reading, the patient was healthy.

## The first leg down

Then, across August and September 2007 — the months of the quant unwind, the first BNP fund gates, the moment credit began whispering — dimension collapsed to roughly **3**. Not a wobble. A two-thirds loss of breathing room in a single season. Twenty stories compressing toward one.

Here is the part that should bother you: **the index went on to make new all-time highs in October 2007.** Price said *fine, better than fine, best ever*. Shape said *the room is closing*. For over a year — through the Bear Stearns rescue, through the summer of false calm — the compression persisted and deepened, until the panel hit its ground state of **2.02** in December 2008. Twenty companies, one trembling object.

If you only watched price, the crisis arrived in September 2008 like weather. If you watched shape, something had been progressively wrong since the summer of 2007. That's not the signature of a lightning strike. That's the signature of a disease: a prodrome, a progression, a collapse that the surface hides until it can't.

## Injury, for contrast

March 2020 shows what the *other* kind of catastrophe looks like. COVID hit the panel with no prodrome at all — dimension didn't erode, it *vanished*, essentially overnight, with mean correlation spiking to 0.765. One day the body was fine; the next it was in the huddle. That's an injury: external, instantaneous, nothing in the shape to warn you because the cause wasn't in the market at all.

The distinction matters because the two catastrophes need different instruments. Nothing on this site will ever see a pandemic coming. But a 2007-style illness — dimension bleeding out over months while prices party — is precisely the kind of thing a weekly vitals check exists to catch.

## What we do about it

The machine encodes the 2007 pattern as a tripwire we call the disease signature: **effective dimension below 75% of its trailing maximum for three consecutive readings.** If it fires, the machine opens a public issue that same Saturday, and you get a letter, because that's the moment the story turns.

And now the honesty paragraph, because this house has one in every room: the disease signature has exactly **one confirming case** — the very episode it was built from. One case is a pattern, not a proof. It may fire on things that aren't diseases; we owe the site a false-alarm study across the full history, and that obligation is registered as a process claim on the [ledger](/ledger/) with a due date, like everything else we owe you. Until it's delivered, hold the tripwire the way you'd hold a promising drug in trials — worth watching, not yet worth betting a diagnosis on.

As of the last reading, dimension sits at 8.68. Wide. Breathing. Nothing like the summer of 2007. Which is exactly what the summer of 2006 looked like, too — and that's why the machine checks every week instead of trusting any single reading, including this one.

A geography professor of mine opened a course by writing three words on the board and then underlining each one separately.

**ALL. MAPS. LIE.**

He wasn't being clever. A map is a flat picture of a round thing, at a scale that fits on a table, drawn by someone who had to decide what mattered. It cannot include everything, and if it tried, it would be the territory and useless. The exclusion is what makes it informative. The lie is not that a map leaves things out. The lie is when it hides *that* it left things out, and *why*.

That sentence is the reason this site exists, and I want to say so plainly.

## A measurement is a map with the time axis removed

Every measurement fixes a thing to the moment it was measured. A thermometer doesn't tell you the fever; it tells you where the fever was at 3:15. The number is true and the fever keeps moving. Anyone who has sat with a sick child knows the difference between the reading and the thing.

A market is a dynamic system — more like weather, or a body, than like a ledger of facts — and nearly every way we're offered to look at it is a still. A close. A headline. A quote on a screen. Each one is accurate about an instant and silent about motion, and motion is the only part that matters if what you want to know is where the thing is going.

## The news is a map, and here is its projection

Watching or listening to the news is a point-in-time measurement of a dynamic system. That's not a complaint; it can't be otherwise. But it's worth naming the rule the map is drawn by, because the rule is never printed in the legend.

The rule is: **draw only what is unusual.**

That is a legitimate projection. It is also a strange one to navigate by. It means the map is made almost entirely of tails — the outliers, the shocks, the one company that did something today — and the body of the distribution, where nearly everything actually lives and slowly moves, is blank paper. Then the next hour draws a different outlier, and the next, and the viewer strings the stills together into a story with a direction. The story never happened in the market. It happened in the sequence of cameras.

So people are surprised by the body. They're surprised that a sector spent five years quietly becoming a fifth of everything, because no single day of that was news. That isn't ignorance. It's the projection working exactly as drawn. Bubbles and crashes are what a living system looks like when you only read it through a stack of stills.

And underneath that is a humbler fact: there is far more happening in the world than anyone knows, and the fraction that reaches the front page is not a sample of the world; it's a sample of the novel. Confusing the second for the first is how a whole culture ends up with a fixed perception of a thing that never stopped moving.

## Our projection

Market Dimensions is also a map, and it also lies. Here is the rule it's drawn by, so you can judge it the way the professor meant.

**We draw the body, not the events.** Twenty stocks, placed by how alike they move, and then time is allowed to run. No single company is the subject. The shape is the subject.

**We draw motion, not position.** The number we care about least is where anything is. The numbers we care about most are deltas: which pairs tightened this week, where the tissue is thickening around a food source, where it is thinning as if the food ran out. A delta is the smallest thing you can report that isn't a still.

**We draw at the scale of a week.** Not an hour, not a year. Slow enough that the daily outliers cancel; fast enough to see a migration while it's underway.

That is the projection. Now the cost, because every projection has one. Mercator's straight lines are a gift to a sailor, and the price is that Greenland looks the size of Africa. Nobody at sea minded.

Ours costs you this:

- **We will be late to shocks.** A sixty-day window takes sixty days to forget. When the body clenches all at once, the news will have it before we do, and by a lot.
- **Single-name stories vanish.** A company's earnings, a scandal, a product — if it doesn't change who moves with whom, it isn't on the map.
- **We can only see correlation.** Not causes, not reasons, not whether the food is real. When two stocks start moving together, the geometry says *that*, not *why*. The *why* is the part a person has to supply, which is what the [weekly verdict](/readings/) is for.
- **Twenty stocks is a small sample of a large body.** We've said [what that costs in noise](/field-guide/what-could-be-wrong/), and we owe you the error bands we haven't published yet.

## Who this map is for

Not for someone who wants to know what happened today. The news does that, and does it well, and I'm not asking anyone to stop watching it. I'm asking them to read it as a projection — a map of the unusual, useful for exactly that — and to keep a second map for the body.

This is the second map. It's for someone who wants to know where the organism is feeding this month and where it will likely be feeding next, and who is willing to be told when we got that wrong, on the [same page](/ledger/) where we said it.

All maps lie. This one tells you how.

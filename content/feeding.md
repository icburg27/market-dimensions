## How to read this

Think of the AI pocket — NVDA and AMD — as the patch of food the organism found two years ago. A slime mold doesn't send one tendril to a food source; the tissue around the source thickens, and you can see the thickening before you can see the mold get bigger. That's what the frontier score measures: how much a stock has started to move *with* the pocket rather than with the rest of the market. High and rising means the organism is feeding through that name. Falling while the price sags means dieback — the food may be running out there, or the organism is rerouting around it.

The third bucket is the one we care about most, because it's the one that tests whether this lens is worth anything. **More food than we thought** are names we had written off as pruned — down hard on the year when the paper experiment opened, so we predicted they'd keep lagging — that are instead outrunning the names we favored. As of this reading, that's VST and CEG, the power producers. If the geometry keeps winning that argument with the prices, it means the correlation structure was carrying information the prices hadn't absorbed. It also means one of our registered predictions is going to grade wrong, which is on the [ledger](/ledger/) already.

## What it isn't

A list of things to buy. The score is computed from prices, so it can't literally see before prices do; what it might do is flag co-movement before *relative return* diverges, and whether it does that better than plain momentum is an open question we've promised to answer. In a clench — when fear pulls every stock toward every other — every score compresses toward zero and this page goes blind. Read it in sunlight. Paper experiment throughout; research and education, not investment advice.

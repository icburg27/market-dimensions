Everything in this guide compresses to three observations. They are not trading rules, and they are not advice. They are the three things we think the geometry says about *when it can be read* — and when it is blind.

## 1. Storms versus sunlight

The geometry cannot see during weather. When the VIX is loud and the dimension is collapsing, it says nothing about the value of any individual thing; it says only that everything has become one thing. That is when it is most tempting to read, and when it says the least. Every reading on this site is labeled sunlight, weather, or storm for that reason, and The Reading will never send you a note during a storm that tells you to do something. Sunlight is when the parts have room to disagree, and the only state in which the frontier detector can see anything at all.

## 2. Exogenous clenches heal fast now

In our count of fourteen crashes since 1929, the time to price recovery has shortened by roughly an order of magnitude — 268 months after 1929, 70 after 1973, 49 after 2008, about five after 2020 (nominal price to prior high, ex-dividends; two episodes carry most of the trend). The shield looks real, but it reads as a reflex, not armor: the internal compression floor has never risen (every episode we can measure still bottoms near dimension two), and prices heal many times faster than structure. In every exogenous clench we measured, prices re-inflated long before the structure did. Whether that holds next time is exactly what a pre-registered claim is for. The residual risk is the scenario where the reflex can't fire.

## 3. Diversification is a fair-weather property

It exists only while the manifold has volume. When the dimension is nine, twenty stocks are twenty bets. When it is two, twenty stocks are one bet, and no reshuffling among them changes that. Which suggests the count of names is not the thing that protects anyone in a clench. What is — that is a question for a licensed professional who knows your situation, not for a geometry.

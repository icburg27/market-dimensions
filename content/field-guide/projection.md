A map is informative because of what it leaves out. The honest map prints its projection in the legend. This is our legend.

Every measurement fixes a thing to the moment it was measured. A market is a dynamic system, so any single reading of it — a close, a headline, a gauge on this site — is a still of something that kept moving. The news is a map drawn on one rule: show what is unusual. It is a map of tails, with the body of the distribution left blank, and if you read a living system only through its outliers you will get bubbles and crashes, because that is what a body looks like in stills. [The longer argument is on the blog.](/blog/all-maps-lie/)

This site is a map drawn on a different rule.

| We draw | We exclude | It costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **The body** — twenty stocks placed by how alike they move | Any single company as a subject | Earnings, scandals, products, and other one-name stories are invisible unless they change who moves with whom |
| **Motion** — which pairs tightened or loosened, where tissue is thickening or thinning | Position — where anything *is* | Nothing here tells you whether something is cheap or expensive |
| **The week** as the unit of time | Hours and headlines | We are late to shocks; a sixty-day window takes sixty days to forget |
| **Correlation** as the raw material | Causes and reasons | The geometry says *that* two things moved together, never *why*; the *why* is what the [human verdict](/readings/) supplies |

Mercator gives a sailor straight lines and makes Greenland look like Africa. Our projection gives you where the organism is feeding and hides nearly everything the front page shows. Both are lies of the useful kind, as long as the legend says so.

Two consequences follow, and the rest of the Field Guide depends on them.

First, the smallest honest thing we can report is a **delta**. A single reading of effective dimension is a still. The change in it week over week is the first number that describes motion instead of a moment. That is why the weekly letter leads with movers, feeding, and dieback, and why the gauges are shown with their week's change beside them.

Second, the still we *do* freeze on purpose is the [ledger](/ledger/). A registered claim pins a moment to a date so it can be graded later. That is a measurement admitting it is one. The difference between the ledger and the news is not that one freezes and the other doesn't; it is that the ledger says so, in advance, with a due date.

If you want the objections to this projection, they are on the [last page](/field-guide/what-could-be-wrong/). If you want to see what it draws this week, start with the [feeding grounds](/field-guide/feeding-grounds/).
